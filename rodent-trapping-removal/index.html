Why placement beats quantity.

A basement scattered with twenty traps does less than three set where an animal really walks. Rodents hug edges, such as the foot of a wall, the top of a joist or the side of a pipe. The technician looks for the greasy smear and the row of pellets that mark that edge.

A device sits right on the run. Snap traps lie at a right angle to the wall, with the trigger end facing the surface the animal rubs. A trap parallel to the route is easy to step around.

Tamper-resistant stations are the form that consumer-grade bait is required to use, and they keep contents away from children, pets and wildlife. Limits on what homeowners can buy are explained on the EPA page linked below.

The logbook and the recheck.

Each device is numbered and marked on the sketch. When the technician returns, the log shows what was set, what was caught and what stayed untouched. A device that has gone quiet is moved, not simply left.

  • Dead animals are removed and bagged, then the device is reset or retired.
  • Bait stations are checked for tampering and refilled only if needed.
  • Devices that caught nothing are relocated based on fresh signs.
  • Final retrieval is checked against the logbook so none remains.

What you can do to improve results.

Remove competing food. A mouse offered a trap and an open bag of cereal will choose the cereal. Store pantry items in sealed tubs, take pet food up at night and empty the trash before bed.

Leave the devices alone. Do not shift them, trigger them out of curiosity or put a pet toy beside them. Report whatever you noticed after the last visit.

A protective checklist for droppings.

You do not need to phone anyone to follow it. Agencies give this advice to anyone who finds droppings indoors.

  • Holding anything dry, be it broom, duster or can of compressed air? Trade it for a spray bottle.
  • Garages, attics and sheds trap stale air, so give them thirty minutes of fresh flow before you step in.
  • Because fingers drift to the face, gloves stay on until the last bag is knotted shut.
  • Soak droppings and urine stains with a household disinfectant or a bleach dilution and let the liquid sit roughly five minutes.
  • A carcass gets lifted with a gloved hand or a shovel, never with bare skin.
  • A stubborn fever, or trouble catching your breath, after exposure deserves a doctor's visit.

About traps and stations.

Snap traps or enclosed stations?

Snap traps work well where the run is known and children and pets are kept out. Enclosed stations suit exposed spots and sensitive households. The technician chooses room by room.

Are glue boards used?

Not as a rule. They snare songbirds, lizards and pets as readily as rodents and offer a slow ending, so they are a last resort.

Why do traps go quiet after a few days?

Early catches take the bold animals. A quiet trap can mean the problem is clearing or that the survivors have learned to avoid the spot, which is why the recheck matters.

Is poison part of the plan?

Poison is not our opening move. A poisoned animal can die between the walls, and in most homes the smell and the danger to pets outweigh any benefit.

How many visits are typical?

Most jobs involve an initial setup and one or two rechecks. The estimate states the planned dates.
