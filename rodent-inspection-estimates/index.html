What the technician does on arrival.

The visit opens with a conversation, not a toolbox. You report what you heard, saw or smelled, and the technician records the room, the time and whatever is odd about the house. Those notes steer the first ten minutes.

A slow lap of the outside comes next. Our tech examines the foundation line, the vents, the gutters, the roof edge and each place a pipe or cable crosses a wall. The inspection then goes indoors, starting in the room where you first saw trouble.

Making sense of what turns up.

Rodents leave a record. The shape of a dropping, the width of a gnaw mark and the direction of a rub smear all say something about the animal and its habits. The technician combines those clues into a short list of the likely routes.

Hint.Possible reading.
Small tapered pellets near food.House mice working a kitchen or pantry.
Larger blunt pellets along a wall base.Norway rats using a ground-level run.
Pale shavings under a rafter.Fresh gnawing in an attic.
Greasy smears at a gap.A regular entry or exit point.
A sweet, heavy smell in one spot.A carcass inside a cavity.

How the written estimate is built.

An estimate is built from jobs and not from hours. One line covers trapping, another covers sealing, and cleanup and repair follow. Every line names its materials and the openings involved, so you can hold it up against what you saw.

  • What the inspection decided about the species.
  • Every entry point found, plus the material meant to close it.
  • The number and style of traps or stations.
  • Cleanup or repair work, priced apart from the rest.
  • Dates for the return visits.

Nothing is bundled to hide a cost. If the cheapest sensible fix is a vent cover and a tube of sealant, the estimate says that.

Getting the house ready.

Before we arrive, make the attic hatch, the door to the crawl space and the garage walls easy to reach. If we are checking the garage, park elsewhere. Keep pets behind a closed door, and write down whatever the dog or cat keeps staring at.

If you have photographs of droppings, chew marks or nests, bring them up on the phone. Pictures from the day you first noticed the problem are often more useful than pictures taken once you have cleaned.

When the answer is not rodents.

Sometimes the visit finds squirrels in a soffit, a loose duct strap or a water pipe tapping as it warms. The report says so, and you are not asked to pay for work you do not need. Near Stillwell Woods Park and similar green space, for example, other wildlife can sound like rodents overhead, which is one reason the walk-through comes first.

Until someone knocks.

  • Leaves or twigs packed into the engine bay mean the car stays parked until it is checked.
  • Carry lawn seed, dog chow and feeder mix indoors for now.
  • Bag one chewed item as proof for whoever comes.
  • Find the breaker panel in case a gnawed cable has to be switched off.

Safer handling while you wait.

Public health sources agree on most points, so the shared ground is boiled down below.

  • Holding anything dry, be it broom, duster or can of compressed air? Trade it for a spray bottle.
  • Make a cross-draft, wait roughly half an hour, then enter.
  • Skin should never touch pellets, urine spots or shredded nest fluff.
  • Wet the ground beneath and around the waste too, since urine reaches further than the stain suggests.
  • Regular trash pickup takes the bag, and any reusable tool gets rinsed.
  • If you fall ill after contact with droppings, that is a medical question, and the clinician needs to know what you handled.

Questions ahead of an inspection.

How long does an inspection take?

The building decides. A small ranch with an open attic goes quickly, and a big house with outbuildings runs longer. Booking comes with an arrival window.

Will I get something in writing?

Yes. The estimate names the species, lists each opening found and prices every step separately, with dates for the return visits.

Do I have to accept everything on the list?

No. Approval happens line by line, and a declined step never blocks the rest of the work.

What if the noise turns out to be something else?

Then the report says so. Plumbing knocks, squirrels and roof-deck movement can mimic rodents, and a trade outside ours may be the right call.

Can an inspection happen the same day?

Often it can, if the calendar has a gap. Someone picks up at every hour, so ask.
