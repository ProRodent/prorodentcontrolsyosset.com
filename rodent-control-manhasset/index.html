Between now and the visit.

  • Shut the door to the loudest room and tell the whole household it is off limits until we come.
  • Dry sweeping is out, because bristles fling grit into the air you breathe.
  • Bait left loose on a shelf can send a dying animal into a wall nobody can open, so leave it in the store.
  • Write the clock time beside every thump overhead.

Before you touch a single pellet.

The federal pages are summarized loosely here, and a doctor, not a pest company, should answer any health question.

  • Dry droppings crumble easily, which keeps the broom and shop vacuum in the closet.
  • Open every opening you can, then spend thirty minutes somewhere else.
  • Peel the gloves off inside out, bag disposable ones, then scrub your hands.
  • Pick up the wet waste with paper towels and no dry rags, and spray again whenever anything looks dusty.
  • Everything the cleanup touched, gloves and towels included, leaves the house in a knotted bag.
  • Fever, body aches or labored breathing in the weeks after cleanup call for a physician, who should be told about the rodent waste. Medical advice is not something we provide.
