Freeport, a South Shore village.

Being a village, it runs a local government, and that can bear on exterior permits and trash rules.

Opening a visit in Freeport.

One village address can hold a house, a detached garage and a little shed all within a few paces, and each gets an equally careful look.

If your lot is near a bay or canal.

Many waterfront lots end up with logs, kelp and snarled limbs after a storm. Piles against siding hide gaps and shelter animals, so haul them away promptly.

Bulkheads, docks and wooden steps close to the house may give animals a covered way to the door. Look at where that route ends.

Work in sequence.

Four stages carry the job: look, trap, seal, recheck. Each has its own purpose, and skipping any of them weakens the rest. Photos of key findings come along with the estimate whenever they help.

Canals, boats and the water's edge.

Stored boat covers and engine bays can hold nests after a quiet winter. Lift them and look before the season starts.

Small clues behind big worries.

Calls usually begin with a single odd detail and a nagging worry. The details below turn up again and again.

  • A thump inside the wall at the same hour each night.
  • A sweet, heavy odor near the baseboard.
  • Grease rings around a pipe where it enters the wall.
  • Shredded tissue tucked in a bathroom cabinet.

What each season does.

A second wave of calls arrives in spring, once people open up attics and sheds and discover what winter left. Look into hatches and corners before you start unpacking.

What the municipality type changes.

Incorporation decides which government sets street rules and the building code. It does not change the hole in your soffit, which our crew can seal either way.

In a village or a hamlet, a mouse still needs only a pencil-wide gap. The label is background.

Dust control before the visit.

The federal pages are summarized loosely here, and a doctor, not a pest company, should answer any health question.

  • Dry droppings crumble easily, which keeps the broom and shop vacuum in the closet.
  • A space that has been shut up needs its doors and windows opened, and then you step away for half an hour.
  • Because fingers drift to the face, gloves stay on until the last bag is knotted shut.
  • Soak droppings and urine stains with a household disinfectant or a bleach dilution and let the liquid sit roughly five minutes.
  • Regular trash pickup takes the bag, and any reusable tool gets rinsed.
  • A stubborn fever, or trouble catching your breath, after exposure deserves a doctor's visit.
