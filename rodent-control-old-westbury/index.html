Old Westbury, a village in two towns.

Incorporated in 1924, Old Westbury is a village that straddles two towns, North Hempstead and Oyster Bay. Its ZIP code, 11568, sits 4.8 miles from Syosset. A homeowner's next step is a call, and the first question we ask is where the sounds come from.

It is a different place from the neighboring Village of Westbury to the south. If your mail says Westbury, check that you are looking at the right page.

Survey, then tools.

Foot traffic means bins at the curb and scraps on the pavement. Tell us where your cans stand and how near they are to the siding.

Four stages in a row.

Right on arrival the tech asks what you noticed and then works from the outside in. Once the routes are clear, devices and repairs are proposed. The return date is fixed before the technician drives away.

A Long Island year.

Fallen leaves pile up along walls and fences and give rodents cover. Rake them away from the foundation and the routes between yard and house become harder to use.

How the type of place shapes the job.

Incorporated villages run their own local government, which can touch permits and building rules. How a rodent enters, and how we close that hole, ignores the label.

Planning new vents or flashing on the outside of the building? Check whether the village wants an approval first. Small repairs usually do not, and the tech can tell you what is typical.

The sign that tipped the caller.

Here are the observations that tend to kick things off.

  • Rustling overhead as the evening air cools.
  • Small footprints crossing a dusty storage shelf.
  • Burrow openings in a flower bed or along a fence.
  • Hoses or wires showing fresh tooth marks.

Two towns, one village.

Because Old Westbury spans two towns, your property may be in one and a neighbor's in the other. That affects tax bills and some permits. It does not affect how a mouse enters a house.

Larger lots mean longer fence lines and more outbuildings. We cannot say what any one property has, so describe the garage, sheds and any guesthouse on the call.

Trees, hedges and the roofline.

Rodents often reach the shed, the detached garage or the play structure before they get to the house. Inspect those first.

Fences work like highways. One running from open ground to your back door deserves a bait station somewhere along its length.

Five habits that keep dust out of your lungs.

This box restates public health advice line by line, and it stays useful whether or not you book a visit.

  • Holding anything dry, be it broom, duster or can of compressed air? Trade it for a spray bottle.
  • Garages, attics and sheds trap stale air, so give them thirty minutes of fresh flow before you step in.
  • Wear rubber, latex or vinyl gloves whenever you handle stained items, and wash your hands when done.
  • For the home mix, the CDC calls for bleach and water at a ratio of 1 to 9, prepared new for every session.
  • One tied bag per session keeps the waste together and the routine simple.
  • Deciding whether symptoms trace back to a cleanup is for a clinician, so get examined if you feel unwell.
