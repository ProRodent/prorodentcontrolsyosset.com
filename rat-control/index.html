Rats are diggers first.

The brown rat, also called the Norway rat, is the species most households in the Northeast meet. It weighs as much as a small pastry bag of flour, digs its own tunnels and prefers to live low. That habit is why a rat visit begins outside, along the fence line, beside the shed and at the edge of the deck.

A burrow mouth is usually a smooth, round hole about the width of a fist, with soil packed hard by travel. Fresh dirt fanned out in front means recent use. Grease-dark smears at the base of a wall mark the same commute above ground.

The first lap outdoors.

Before touching the house, the technician walks the lot and logs every food source and every hiding place. The log becomes the plan, because killing rats without removing what feeds them just opens a vacancy.

  • Bird feeders and the seed that spills beneath them.
  • Open compost bins or garbage cans without tight lids.
  • Woodpiles stacked directly on the ground.
  • Dog bowls, grill drippings and fallen fruit.
  • Dense ivy, tall shrubs or clutter against the foundation.

Placing traps where rats actually walk.

Rats are wary of new objects, so devices are set along established runs and sometimes left unarmed for a day or two to let the animals get used to them. Snap traps sit in covered boxes, and enclosed stations are anchored where wind, pets and children cannot disturb them.

Rat jobs usually need more patience than mouse jobs. Early catches are normal, and then the traps go quiet. That silence is the cue to confirm the entry points with the sealing work before declaring the problem solved.

What you can do before we arrive.

Pick up fallen fruit and pet food, lock down the garbage lids and move firewood onto a rack. Do not pour bait into burrows. Rats that eat it may die under a deck or inside a wall, and the odor is far worse than the original problem.

Wooded edges and park borders.

Stillwell Woods Park is named in local research, though we hold no facts about its layout. If your lot backs onto woods, a trail or any open green space, tell the person on the phone. Fallen leaves and limbs near a house give rats a hidden approach, and a band of bare soil or gravel next to the foundation removes it. A technician can suggest which side of the house to clear first.

Holding pattern until we arrive.

  • Leaves or twigs packed into the engine bay mean the car stays parked until it is checked.
  • Carry lawn seed, dog chow and feeder mix indoors for now.
  • Bag one chewed item as proof for whoever comes.
  • Find the breaker panel in case a gnawed cable has to be switched off.

Before you touch a single pellet.

This box restates public health advice line by line, and it stays useful whether or not you book a visit.

  • Dry droppings crumble easily, which keeps the broom and shop vacuum in the closet.
  • Airing out a closed room for about thirty minutes is the first CDC move.
  • Skin should never touch pellets, urine spots or shredded nest fluff.
  • Pick up the wet waste with paper towels and no dry rags, and spray again whenever anything looks dusty.
  • A carcass gets lifted with a gloved hand or a shovel, never with bare skin.
  • Our skill is pest work, which means sickness belongs with a health professional.

Rat questions from Nassau households.

Norway rat or roof rat?

A Norway rat is stocky, short-tailed and a digger that stays low. A roof rat is slimmer, long-tailed and a climber, and in the Northeast it is rarer. Pellets, burrows and how high the noise comes from tell the technician which one it is.

Do mulch beds make burrowing easier?

Soft soil and loose mulch tunnel easily, and heavy rain can shift a burrow that runs along a foundation. Loose soil, smooth entrances and trodden trails matter more than old holes, and that is what the technician hunts for.

Is cleaning up after rats overhead risky?

Fine grit rising from dry pellets is the hazard. The box on this page carries the CDC steps, and a technician should deal with any large mess.

Will trimming my shrubs help?

Thick shrubs against siding shelter rats for the last few feet of a trip, and trimming them shortens that shelter. Trimming does nothing for a gap in a soffit or foundation, which makes both jobs matter.

Do rats ever leave on their own?

Rarely while food and shelter remain. Without a change outside and a sealed entry inside, the pattern repeats.

Details for a rat booking.

Mention feeders, compost, wood stacks, decks and sheds near the house. Those objects tell the technician where to start the first lap.
