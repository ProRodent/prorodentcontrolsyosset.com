A protective checklist for droppings.

Dry droppings and nest scraps can carry germs. Slow, damp work is the safe route, and the CDC pages linked at the end support each step.

  • Even a soft dust mop flings old pellets upward, so no dry tool starts the job.
  • Plan on thirty minutes of ventilation before any cleaning begins.
  • Skin should never touch pellets, urine spots or shredded nest fluff.
  • Wet the ground beneath and around the waste too, since urine reaches further than the stain suggests.
  • Everything the cleanup touched, gloves and towels included, leaves the house in a knotted bag.
  • Fever, body aches or labored breathing in the weeks after cleanup call for a physician, who should be told about the rodent waste. Medical advice is not something we provide.
