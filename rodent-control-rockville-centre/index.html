Low-lying lots and what they mean.

Salt air rusts things silently. From the ground a vent screen can look intact while its rim has rotted away to a gap as wide as a pencil. Coated or stainless mesh outlasts plain steel.

Low sandy ground drains quickly and digs easily. After heavy rain a burrow beside the foundation can shift, which is why the tech looks for fresh soil instead of old holes.

The sign that tipped the caller.

Nobody plans to hire a rodent technician. A small shock does it, and these are the usual shocks.

  • Rustling overhead as the evening air cools.
  • Small footprints crossing a dusty storage shelf.
  • Burrow openings in a flower bed or along a fence.
  • Hoses or wires showing fresh tooth marks.

Trouble in the off months.

Cycles of freezing and thawing loosen trim and sealant, and that opens new gaps. Looking over the exterior each spring and fall catches them before animals do.

This box restates public health advice line by line, and it stays useful whether or not you book a visit.
