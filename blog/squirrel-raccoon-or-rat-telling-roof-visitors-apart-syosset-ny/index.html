Squirrel, Raccoon or Rat: Telling Roof Visitors Apart

Not every animal on the roof is a rodent, and the right fix depends on who it is. Sound, timing and droppings narrow it down.

Something is on the roof. Is it a squirrel running the gutters, a raccoon denning in the attic or a rat using a tree limb as a bridge? The answers are different, and so are the fixes. This article gives you a few clues to sort them out.

Timing.

Squirrels move mainly by day, with peaks early in the morning and late in the afternoon. Raccoons keep to the night, though a mother with young may be heard at odd hours. Rats and mice are largely nocturnal and become busiest once the house goes quiet.

Sound.

  • Quick scampering and chattering suggests squirrels.
  • Heavy thumps, dragging or low growls point to raccoons.
  • Light scratching, rustling or gnawing suggests mice or rats.
  • Fluttering and chirping near a vent suggests birds.

Sound can mislead. A single rat can sound large in a hollow space. Always combine sound with other signs.

Droppings and signs.

Mouse.About a quarter inch, pointed.Tiny gnaw marks, grease smears.

Squirrel.Larger, rounded, tan to brown.Nut shells, chewed wood.

Where each animal enters.

Mice and rats use small gaps. Squirrels chew larger holes at roof edges and vents. Raccoons can tear off loose boards or lift a vent cover. The sealing page describes materials that resist all three.

Who to call.

For mice and rats, a rodent technician is the right call. For squirrels and raccoons, a wildlife specialist who handles live capture and exclusion is often better. If you are unsure, call us, describe the clues and we will tell you honestly whether the job is ours. A visit starts with an inspection either way.

Safety first.

A raccoon can bite and can carry disease. Do not corner or touch any wild animal. Call animal control about an injured or strange-acting animal, and keep pets and children away from the attic and the roof.

Putting the clues together.

No single clue settles the question, so collect several. Write down the time of day, the size of the sounds and the kind of marks you can see from the ground. A daytime thump on a branch near the roof points one way, whereas a nighttime shuffle near the soffit points another. Photographs of any droppings, with a coin beside them for scale, are worth more than a verbal description.

Next, look at the weather. Squirrels are most active in the morning and late afternoon and tend to get busier when nuts and acorns drop. Raccoons often move heavy objects on a roof and raise a ruckus around the time they settle in. Rats keep to a nighttime schedule and usually leave smears along the routes they use.

Finally, consider the season. A female squirrel or raccoon looking for a nursery site may choose an attic in late winter or spring, which makes the problem urgent because young animals will be inside. If you suspect that, avoid sealing anything yourself. A closed hole with a family inside leads to scratching, odor and bigger repairs.

When you call, share your notes and photographs. Ask whether the technician handles the species you suspect, and what happens with the animals once they are out. Wildlife removal can be a separate trade from rodent work, so a straight answer about scope is a good sign.

Dust control before the visit.

You do not need to phone anyone to follow it. Agencies give this advice to anyone who finds droppings indoors.

  • Even a soft dust mop flings old pellets upward, so no dry tool starts the job.
  • Airing out a closed room for about thirty minutes is the first CDC move.
  • Peel the gloves off inside out, bag disposable ones, then scrub your hands.
  • For the home mix, the CDC calls for bleach and water at a ratio of 1 to 9, prepared new for every session.
  • A carcass gets lifted with a gloved hand or a shovel, never with bare skin.
  • If illness strikes within a few weeks, bring up the rodent contact with your doctor.

Worries we hear most.

Do you handle raccoons and squirrels?

Our focus is mice and rats. If the animal is something else, the report says so and suggests a wildlife specialist.

Are squirrels rodents?

Yes, technically. They are different in habit and handling.

What if I hear daytime noise?

That points to squirrels or birds more than mice or rats.

How can I tell by droppings?

Squirrel droppings are larger and rounder than rat droppings. Raccoon droppings are bigger still and often in a latrine.

What do I do if I see a raccoon in the attic?

Do not approach. Call a wildlife specialist.
