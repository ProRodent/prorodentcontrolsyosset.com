Curb Day Habits: Bins, Bags and Overnight Visitors

The night before pickup is when a lot of bags and bins sit outside. Rodents notice. A few small habits change what they find, and cost nothing but attention.

Waste is a fact of every household, and how you handle it decides what comes to your yard at night. Mice and rats are scavengers. They do not need a feast, just a reliable crumb, and a bin that fails once a week can feed a family of them.

This article walks through the weekly cycle of trash, recycling and compost, with the changes that help most.

The bin itself.

A good bin has a tight lid, sturdy sides and a base that does not tip. Wheeled bins with hinged lids work well. Open buckets, torn bags and bins with cracked lids do not.

  • Lids should sit flat and latch or have a weight on them.
  • Replace bins with cracks, chewed holes or broken hinges.
  • Keep bins on a hard surface, not directly on soil or mulch.
  • Clean the inside every few weeks to remove residue.
  • Put a bungee cord or latch on bins that raccoons open.

Timing and placement.

Place bins away from the house if you can, since animals that find food near a wall will explore the wall. A spot beside the garage or at the edge of the driveway, not touching siding, is better than a corner against the foundation.

Put bags out in the morning of pickup, not the night before. If your schedule requires the night before, use a sturdy bin with a lid and avoid leaving loose bags on the ground.

Food waste and kitchen habits.

Food scraps are the main draw. Wrap meat trimmings and fish waste, freeze them if you can until pickup day and drain wet waste before bagging. Rinse cans, jars and bottles.

Compost done carefully.

An enclosed compost bin with a hardware cloth floor keeps rodents out better than an open pile. Add brown material such as leaves along with kitchen scraps, turn it regularly and avoid meat, dairy, oils and cooked food.

If you already have an open pile and see burrows beneath it, do not dig it up. Call, and the technician can advise on a safe way to move it.

Shared bins and apartment buildings.

In a multi-unit building, a dumpster serves many households. One overfull dumpster can feed animals that then explore the building. Report overflowing bins to the manager, and ask for lids to be kept closed.

For businesses, the commercial page explains how dumpsters and delivery doors are handled in a written report.

A weekly routine.

  • Monday: wash the bin if it smells and check the lid.
  • Midweek: rinse recycling and drain wet waste.
  • Night before pickup: bring in anything that could spill.
  • Morning of pickup: put bins out and close lids.
  • After pickup: bring bins back and rinse if needed.

None of this removes rodents already in the house. It does make your yard a less rewarding stop, which helps all the other measures work.

  • Garages, attics and sheds trap stale air, so give them thirty minutes of fresh flow before you step in.
  • Thin disposable gloves suit small messes, while heavier rubber pairs suit big ones and can be rinsed.

Worries we hear most.

Should I put bags out the night before?

If you can, put them out in the morning. Bags left overnight are an easy meal.

Do lids really matter?

A tight lid is the best single step. Rodents can gnaw plastic, but they rarely bother with a lid that fits well.

What about recycling bins?

Rinse food containers. Residue attracts rodents and insects.

Is compost allowed?

Yes, in an enclosed bin with a floor of hardware cloth. Avoid meat, dairy and cooked food.

How do I clean a bin that smells?

Wash it with hot water and a mild disinfectant, then dry it in the sun. Smells draw animals.
