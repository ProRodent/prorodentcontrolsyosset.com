Weather and rodent timing.

Fallen leaves pile up along walls and fences and give rodents cover. Rake them away from the foundation and the routes between yard and house become harder to use.

Why the label means little.

A village counts as a municipality in its own right. A landlord or building owner might have to approve work, and the village may regulate changes to the exterior.

Planning new vents or flashing on the outside of the building? Check whether the village wants an approval first. Small repairs usually do not, and the tech can tell you what is typical.

Suburban lots and rodent cover.

A woodpile resting on the ground against a wall works like a hotel. Move it to a rack some feet from the house and the fuel stays while the guests do not.

Large trees tend to lean across a roof, and a limb that rubs the shingles serves as a bridge. Pruning it counts among the cheapest rodent jobs available.
