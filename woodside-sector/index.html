Trees, limbs and the roofline.

We cannot say how many trees stand on any street in this part of Syosset, and some lots may have few. If yours has mature trees near the house, a few general points apply. A limb within a few feet of the roof gives a squirrel, rat or mouse a bridge, and an overhanging branch that scrapes a soffit can widen a gap.

  • Look at where branches touch the roof, the gutters or the siding.
  • Check the soffit and fascia below for torn screens or dark smears.
  • Rake leaves and mulch back from the foundation.
  • Cut a gap of a few feet between tree and roof where you safely can.

Tree work near rooflines is a job for a professional, not a ladder. A technician notes what is touching your house and tells you which limbs matter most.

Fall, leaves and the move indoors.

As nights cool, mice and rats look for warmth. In wooded, leafy neighborhoods that often coincides with leaf drop, which provides cover along walls. We make no claim that calls rise on a particular street. We do know that closing gaps before the first hard frost is easier than doing it in January.

  • Walk the lot in early fall and look at the base of every wall.
  • Pour bird seed and pet food into lidded tubs.
  • Move firewood onto a rack away from the wall.
  • Check the garage door seal for daylight.

While you wait for the technician.

Do not disturb droppings, keep kids and pets out of the room and avoid putting out bait. Poison can lead to an animal dying inside a wall, and glue boards harm other wildlife. Should cleaning not wait, the CDC box below shows how to handle waste safely.

Tell us the room, the hour and what you heard, saw or smelled. A short, concrete account helps more than a long one.

How a visit works here.

The crew starts with the newest evidence, walks the outside, lists the openings and sets devices on the real runs. Repairs wait until the early catches show which entries are active, and a second visit verifies them. The sequence is the same across Syosset, adjusted for trees, wind and the way your house is used.

Do this meanwhile.

  • Shut the door to the loudest room and tell the whole household it is off limits until we come.
  • Dry sweeping is out, because bristles fling grit into the air you breathe.
  • Bait left loose on a shelf can send a dying animal into a wall nobody can open, so leave it in the store.
  • Write the clock time beside every thump overhead.

Before you touch a single pellet.

The federal pages are summarized loosely here, and a doctor, not a pest company, should answer any health question.

  • Dry droppings crumble easily, which keeps the broom and shop vacuum in the closet.
  • Fresh air does part of the work, so let the room breathe while empty.
  • Wear rubber, latex or vinyl gloves whenever you handle stained items, and wash your hands when done.
  • Soak the spot, leave it alone for a few minutes, then collect it with paper towels bound for the trash.
  • A sealed bag keeps stray particles from trailing you through the house.
  • A stubborn fever, or trouble catching your breath, after exposure deserves a doctor's visit.
